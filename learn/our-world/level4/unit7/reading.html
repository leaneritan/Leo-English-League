🌟 Young and Creative
📌 Paragraph 1 — Introduction
Did you know that a teenager had the first idea for a television? And a six-year-old boy invented the toy truck? Kids and teens are great inventors because they have a lot of creativity and imagination 🌈.
🔦 Paragraph 2 — Ann Makosinski (2013, age 15)
Canadian high school student Ann Makosinski was 15 when she invented the "hollow flashlight." The flashlight uses heat from the human body and changes it into light. It doesn't need batteries 🔋. Ann thought of the idea when she heard about a friend who lived in the Philippines. Her friend was doing badly in school. She needed to do her homework at night, but there was no electrical light in her home. Ann's flashlight isn't expensive. Ann hopes it will help some of the 1.1 billion people in the world who still have no electricity ⚡ in their homes.
🍦 Paragraph 3 — Frank Epperson (1905, age 11)
When Frank Epperson was eleven, he left a cup filled with soda and a stick in his yard. That night he forgot about it. It was a very cold night. When he went outside the next morning, he found something amazing: a "Popsicle"!
📖 Paragraph 4 — Louis Braille (1824, age 15)
Louis Braille had an accident when he was three. The accident left him blind 👁️. At that time, it was hard for blind children to read. They had to touch raised letters. But it was easy to confuse a Q with an O, an R with a B, and so on. When he was fifteen, Louis invented an alphabet that used raised dots. The Braille alphabet was a big success!
🏙️ Paragraph 5 — Angad Daryani (2018, age 19)
At the age of 19, Angad Daryani invented a solution 🔑 to the problem ⚠️ of air pollution in cities. Growing up in Mumbai, India, he suffered from asthma caused by the polluted air. Air pollution is a big problem. Three millions 🔢 of people die each year. Angad's invention 💡 is an air pollution cleaning tower that is 6.1 meters (20 ft.) high. The tower sucks in air and takes out the particles of dust and carbon. The particles are collected and can even be reused. Angad's dream is to build thousands of the towers and improve the quality of air in cities.
Leo 💬 "Wow! All four inventors were young — like 11, 15, 15, and 19 years old!"
Dad 💬 "That's right! And each one had a problem to solve. Now let's listen again in the next module."

Think Aloud

📘 Reading Strategy: Ask Questions
Ask Questions helps you understand and remember a text. Ask questions before, during, and after you read.

Use Who · What · Where · When · Why · How
🎙️ Dad Models the Strategy
Dad 💬 "Leo, watch how I use 'Ask Questions' while I read. I'll think out loud so you can hear how I do it!"
🔍 Step 1 — Before Reading
Dad 💬 "I look at the title 'Young and Creative.' I ask: Who are these young people? What did they create? This gives me a purpose for reading!"
❓ Step 2 — During Reading (Paragraph 2)
Dad 💬 "I read: 'Angad suffered from asthma.' I'm not sure what asthma means. I ask: What is asthma? Are there any clues nearby?"
💡 Step 3 — Find the Clue
Dad 💬 "The next sentence says: 'the asthma was caused by the polluted air.' Now I think — asthma must be a problem with your breathing when the air is dirty!"
✅ Step 4 — After Reading
Dad 💬 "I ask: What is the main idea? Which parts are unclear? This helps me check my understanding."
Leo 💬 "So asking questions before, during, and after helps me understand better!"
🏆 Graphic Literacy — The Patent Graph
Patent 📜 — An official paper that says a certain inventor is responsible for an invention. If you have a patent, nobody can steal your idea!
Dad 💬 "Leo, look at the line graph on page 125. The numbers at the bottom show the years (2000–2006). The numbers on the left show how many patents were filed. Ask yourself: Which year had the most patents?"
